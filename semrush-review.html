Semrush, reviewed

Is the SEO toolkit everyone recommends actually worth the subscription? Here's where it earns it, and where it doesn't.

Semrush has been the default answer to "what SEO tool should I use" for years, and in 2026 that's still mostly deserved — though it's grown into a sprawling platform that most people only use a fraction of. Adobe acquired Semrush in April 2026, which hasn't changed the product much yet but is worth knowing if you're evaluating its long-term direction.

Where it actually earns its keep

The Keyword Magic Tool is the feature people stick around for. Type in a single seed term and it returns thousands of related keywords pulled from a database of over 25 billion, each tagged with search volume, difficulty, cost-per-click, and search intent, then grouped into topic clusters automatically. For anyone planning content rather than guessing at it, that clustering alone saves real time.

Site Audit is the other feature worth the price on its own. It crawls your entire site, checks for more than 140 technical SEO issues, and ranks fixes by actual impact rather than dumping an undifferentiated list on you. If you've ever stared at Google Search Console wondering why a page won't index, this is the tool that tells you why.

What's new this year

Semrush added an AI Visibility Toolkit in 2026 that tracks whether your brand shows up in answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews — a real response to search shifting away from the traditional ten blue links. It's a genuinely useful add-on if your traffic depends on being found through AI-powered search, though it's priced and sold separately from the core plans, so factor that in before assuming it's included.

Where it falls short

It's a lot of tool. Most users end up living in two or three features (usually keyword research and site audit) while paying for a suite that includes PPC research, social scheduling, and local SEO tools they never open. If your rival Ahrefs is on your shortlist too, Ahrefs generally has the stronger backlink database — Semrush's edge is breadth and the newer AI-visibility tracking, not link analysis specifically.

The verdict

Worth it if you're actively managing a site's SEO and will use the keyword research and audit tools weekly — the time saved justifies the cost fast. Skip it if you just need the occasional keyword idea; that's overkill and overpriced for casual use.
